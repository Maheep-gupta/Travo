| D-015 | 2026-11-02 | Out-of-order session writes (schema v3, spec 4.2 "newer wins"):<br>• Every connector session write carries `observed_at`.<br>• The database clamps it to `now() + 5 min` and rejects a null.<br>• `sessions_guard_update` silently skips an update older than the stored observation (the row is left out of RETURNING). Equal times are applied.<br>• The connector records no event, messages or known state for skipped rows and retries next cycle.<br>• Events rely on the existing `(session_id, dedupe_key)` key; there is no ordering check in the database.<br>• Clients require exactly schema v3.<br>• If session writes are skipped, or fail after a good heartbeat, for `SKIPPED_WRITES_LIMIT` (3) cycles in a row, the connector's heartbeat reports the source as not responding, so the dashboard stops trusting statuses that would otherwise look current (security review M1, QA M2).<br>• v3 grants UPDATE on `host_id`, `provider` and `external_id`. PostgREST upserts set every payload column on conflict, and `sessions_guard_update` rejects any change to them (security review M2; unverified until `test:db` runs). | Late writes come from overlapping connector processes, delayed requests or a clock that stepped back. Skipping rather than rejecting keeps one late row from failing the whole batch and falsely reporting the backend offline. Clamping rather than rejecting keeps a fast-clock host working, and limits the freeze after its clock is fixed to 5 minutes. No project has applied v2 publicly, and v2 can't accept the new field. | A provider supplies its own event times; multi-writer hosts are supported; the RLS tests show the trigger behaving differently |
